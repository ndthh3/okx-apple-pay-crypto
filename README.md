Apple Pay itself adds a layer OKX never sees: tokenisation. The exchange receives a one-time encrypted transaction code rather than your 16-digit card number, so a breach on the platform side doesn't expose your card. Crypto normally lands in your OKX wallet within minutes of a confirmed payment.

## Why the fee is higher than a bank transfer

Purchases made with Apple Pay run through the card rail, not the bank transfer rail, and card processing carries costs that ACH or SEPA transfers don't. OKX's own guidance says card transactions typically cost slightly more than bank transfers for that reason, and that the platform displays a full breakdown before you confirm. That's the number that matters, because it's calculated on your actual order size, currency and card.

OKX doesn't publish a headline rate for this payment method, so treat any specific percentage you see attributed to OKX with suspicion unless it's on the preview screen in front of you. For calibration, Crypto.com states a 2.99% processing fee on card purchases and requires you to tick a box accepting it, while Okcoin, a separate platform from OKX, lists 2.49%. Card purchases generally land in that 2.5% to 3% neighbourhood across the industry.

Limits work the same way: they're set by OKX as daily or weekly purchase caps, and separately by your card issuer on the card itself. Both apply at once. If you're moving a large amount, a bank transfer or wire is the cheaper and less friction-prone route, and OKX says as much in its own educational material.

## After you buy: withdrawal holds and declined charges

Two post-purchase behaviours cause most of the support tickets.

**You can't cash out to Apple Pay.** OKX only processes withdrawals to a linked bank account. Apple Pay and Google Pay are not withdrawal destinations, on OKX or anywhere else in this category, because they're payment rails rather than deposit accounts.

**Your coins may be temporarily locked from withdrawal.** OKX states that some Apple Pay purchases are subject to a temporary withdrawal hold as part of its security protocols. You can buy instantly, but the assets may sit in the account for a period before they can be moved off-platform. Plan around that if you were buying to send somewhere else immediately.

**A declined order isn't necessarily a lost payment.** When a card transaction is declined, the issuing bank often places a temporary authorisation hold on the amount at the moment of the attempt. That hold usually releases automatically within a few days, depending on the bank's processing time. If it's still there after a week, OKX recommends contacting your bank rather than the exchange, since the bank controls the hold.

## FAQ

**Why can't I see Apple Pay as a payment method?**
Three possible reasons, and OKX lists all of them: you're on web or Android, your account isn't verified, or your country isn't in the supported list. A fourth, subtler case is that Apple Pay's own country and device support doesn't line up with your OKX verified country.

**Can I use a credit card, or does it have to be a debit card?**
Both work. OKX accepts Visa and Mastercard credit or debit cards. Your issuer can still block the transaction at its own discretion, which is why some buyers find one card fails and another goes through.

**Is there a minimum or maximum purchase?**
OKX doesn't publish a figure for Apple Pay in its help documentation. Expect two ceilings to apply simultaneously: an OKX purchase limit and whatever your bank allows on the card. Large orders belong on a bank transfer.

**Can I withdraw the crypto immediately?**
Not always. Some Apple Pay purchases trigger a temporary withdrawal hold under OKX's security protocols. The purchase itself settles in minutes; the ability to move the coins off the platform may lag.

**My transaction was declined but the money left my account. Where is it?**
It's almost certainly a temporary authorisation hold placed by your bank at the moment of the attempt, not a completed charge. These normally clear within a few days. After a week, call your bank.
